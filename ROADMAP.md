## 0.2 — images

- [x] Anisotropic TV in 2D and 3D (2026-10-16): `fp_op_tv_aniso`, `TVAniso`, `tv_aniso`. Two active axes: Beck's accelerated dual block method (ADBPG, §12.5.4, Thm 12.19; Chambolle–Pock 2015) with exact 1D TV steps; three: Beck's fast dual proximal gradient for three blocks (Algorithm 9), which beat parallel proximal Dykstra at every accuracy. Certified gap stopping, warm starts through the workspace dual, polish to the exact solution on the identified regions, and O(n) derivatives from those regions. On Apple M5 fastest from 1e-3 relative error on in 2D against proxTV's DR2, Kolmogorov2, Yang2 and parallel Dykstra, 3.0–3.7× faster than the best proxTV solver at 1e-6 ([docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md))
- [x] Anisotropic TV in 3D at high accuracy (2026-10-17): gradient restarts (O'Donoghue and Candès) in both solvers, and the polish rebuilt to take its regions from the dual (edges whose 1D dual is strictly inside its bound are flat at the solution). It now runs at the gap checks and stops the solve once the polished point certifies, with a gap at most 1/100 of the iterate's. On 64³ the time to 1e-6 relative error fell 4.7–5.6×, and fastprox is now 1.8× (λ = 0.1) and 4.8× (λ = 0.5) faster than Yang's ADMM, fastest at every accuracy. 2D to 1e-6 is 2.3–2.8× faster than before. The nested split (exact 1D on one axis, warm-started 2D ADBPG on the other two, as in Barbero–Sra's proximity stacking) was tried first and lost to the restarted three-block method at every accuracy ([docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md))
- [x] Feasible warm starts for anisotropic TV (2026-10-17): a starting dual is checked fiber by fiber and rebuilt from its clipped running sums where it is infeasible; before, a seed such as q₀ = x − mean(x) certified the constant image at 0 iterations
- [x] Anisotropic TV to 1e-2 in 2D at small λ (2026-10-17): the gap is checked on the second block step of an iteration, one sweep instead of a 1D pass, at iterations scheduled from the gap's rate of fall, and 2D polishes are retried early only on a promising ratio. At λ = 0.1, 1e-2 is reached 1.8–2.0× sooner and 1.6–1.7× faster than DR2; fastprox is now fastest at every accuracy on every benchmark image. 1e-3 is up to 22% faster than before; 1e-4 at λ = 0.1 is still 11–12% slower than with z(y) as the iterate ([docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md))
- [x] Parallel polish for anisotropic TV (2026-10-17): union-find in fixed slabs along axis 0 (at most 64, of at least 4 planes or rows), each joined by its own thread, then the crossing edges serially; labels and region sums per slab, the crossing components serially. The output is bit-identical to the serial polish. The edge pass had not vectorised (its pointers lost `restrict` through a lambda) and is 5.6× faster. One polish on 64³: 7.1 → 6.5 ms on one thread, 3.9 → 1.8 ms on eight; whole solves to 1e-12 gain 1–6% on one thread and 3–15% on eight ([docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md))
- [x] Isotropic TV in 2D and 3D (2026-10-16): `fp_op_tv_iso`, `TVIso`, `tv_iso`. Fast gradient projection on the dual (Beck–Teboulle 2009; Beck 2017, §12.4.4) with step 1/L, L = 4 Σ wₐ² (8 in 2D, 12 in 3D), primal from the last dual iterate (12.19), gap stopping certifying ‖z − z*‖² ≤ 2·gap (Lemma 12.7), dual returned and accepted for warm starts. Per-axis weights inside the norm. Derivatives by conjugate gradients on the flat regions, with the residual reported. Against scikit-image's Chambolle projection on Apple M5: 2.6–11× faster from 1e-3 relative error on, and the only one of the two to reach 1e-6; scikit-image is 25–45% faster to 1e-2 at small γ ([docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md))
- [x] Isotropic TV at high accuracy (2026-10-17): gradient restarts (O'Donoghue and Candès, Found. Comput. Math. 15, 2015), tested every 4th iteration with a non-shrinking interval, take up to 15× fewer iterations to 1e-8 and below; the derivative CG is Jacobi-preconditioned (1.4–3× fewer iterations where it is expensive). The rate does not become linear, because the dual is degenerate. A Newton polish on the flat regions was tried and was worse than z(u) ([docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md))
- [x] 1D TV with ℓ2 of differences (2026-10-17): `fp_op_tv1d_l2`, `TV1DL2`, `tv1d_l2`. Newton on the secular equation of the dual trust-region problem (Moré–Sorensen; Nocedal–Wright Alg. 4.4) instead of projected Newton, exact to rounding in 2–8 tridiagonal steps, with closed-form derivatives; 1.0–1.3× faster than proxTV's `more_TV2`, which hits its iteration cap on many large rows ([docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md))
- [x] Nonconvex pointwise maps (2026-10-16): ℓ0, ℓq (closed forms for q = 1/2 and 2/3, Newton from |x| otherwise, monotone on the nonzero branch), SCAD and MCP for any step (a > 1 + γ, b > γ, else `FP_EPARAM`), log penalty. - [x] Derivatives for the nonconvex maps (diagonal, support-restricted, from the scalar equation; the selected branch at ties) (2026-10-16)

## 0.3 — graph TV

- [x] Max-flow engine (2026-10-17): `src/maxflow/maxflow.hpp`, two search trees with growth, augmentation and adoption written from Section 3 of Boykov and Kolmogorov (TPAMI 2004), first in first out with the distance marks of Section 3.3; the trees and the flow survive changes of terminal and edge residuals (`add_terminal`, `set_edge`), repaired as in Kohli and Torr (TPAMI 2007). The graph is built once; solves run in caller memory and never allocate. Tested in `tests/c/test_maxflow.cpp` by a full certificate after every solve (feasible flow, conservation, the trees equal to the residual reachability sets, cut capacity equal to the flow), against Dinic's algorithm, and reused solves against fresh ones node for node, including the graph TV level pattern. On TV cut problems one cut is 1.6–11× faster than Dinic, except 64³ at γ = 0.5 (0.7×) (`bench/bench_maxflow.cpp`, docs/benchmarks.md)
- [x] Graph TV by parametric max-flow (2026-10-17): `fp_op_graph_tv`, `fp.GraphTV`; each part cut at the mean of its solution (exact when the cut is trivial), clamped into the middle half of its interval, all parts in one solve per round with the flow and trees reused; exact to rounding at `tol = 0`, else within `tol`·(max x − min x). Gap from the flow as the dual; exact region derivatives. Tested by an independent KKT check, against 1D TV on chains, anisotropic TV on grids (to 1e-7, the iterative solver's own accuracy) and CVXPY fixtures. On 512² 4-neighbour grids it takes about 20 solves, 0.13 s at γ = 0.02 to 3–4 s at γ = 0.5 on one thread, against 0.09–1.7 s for TVAniso at a gap tolerance of 1e-10 (errors up to 1e-5); exact graph TV is faster than that at γ = 0.1, slower at γ = 0.02 and 0.5. The time is in max-flow, which re-routes flow through the flat regions every round. kNN graphs of 10⁵ points take 0.15–0.65 s. Starting the rounds from the exact dual takes the max-flow part from 5.5 s to 0.12 s, but approximate duals (up to 400 FISTA iterations) gave nothing: the dual is far from unique and a flow close in value is not close in shape (docs/benchmarks.md)
- [x] TV on trees and chains (2026-10-18): `fp_op_tree_tv`, `fp.TreeTV`, graph TV's arguments for edges that form a forest; Algorithm 2 and Section 4.2 of Kolmogorov, Pock and Rolínek (SIAM J. Imaging Sci. 9, 2016), the breakpoints of the messages in two pairing heaps (O(n log n)), or a sorted deque when every tree is a path (Section 4.1, O(n)); values recomputed as region means, the dual flow from the subtree sums, graph TV's gap and derivatives (shared in `src/ops/edge_tv.hpp`). Tested against exact graph TV, against 1D TV on shuffled paths, by the KKT check and CVXPY fixtures. On 10⁶ nodes: 70–190 ms for random trees, 35–75 ms for a comb spanning tree of a 1000² image, 4–8× faster than graph TV; paths 22–27 ms, 3.5–4× the time of `TV1D` on the ordered signal (docs/benchmarks.md)
- [x] VJPs for the iterative operators by conjugate gradients on the dual active set, returning the residual. Choose the active set from the strict support of the computed primal and handle ties within the tolerance explicitly: picking an index set by rule can give a matrix outside the generalised Jacobian (Outrata, Kočvara and Zowe, *Nonsmooth Approach to Optimization Problems with Equilibrium Constraints*, Ex. 7.3) — Done in substance (2026-10-18), and closed: isotropic TV's VJP solves its system by conjugate gradients on the flat regions read off the dual and reports the residual (`fp_workspace_deriv_info`, `ctx.deriv`), stopped by default at the certified error of z; anisotropic, graph and tree TV have exact region averages; ties within a tolerance are reported by the kink masks and resolved explicitly by the branch overrides (item below)
- [x] Kink and tie mask (the elementwise part with kinks is done, see 0.2; ties, the other operators and the image operators remain): report which entries sat at a kink of the prox or on a tie, not only the count in `fp_info.ties`. The elementwise operators come first, in 0.2 (see there). Kinks are the biactive entries of each operator: |xᵢ| = γλ for soft thresholding, the breakpoints of SCAD and MCP, an entry exactly on a bound of the box, an entry at the simplex threshold, a 1D TV edge whose dual sits exactly at ±γwₑ, a group whose norm equals its threshold; for the image operators, the pixels where the dual is on the boundary of its ball with a zero jump. An optional tolerance flags near-kinks as well, since exact equality is rare in floating point; the iterative VJPs above need the same rule for ties within the tolerance. It crosses the C API through the workspace like `fp_workspace_deriv_info`, written only with `keep_structure` (`fp_prox` keeps its signature), and in Python as `ctx.kinks`. `jvp` and `vjp` take an optional branch override for the flagged entries, so a caller can build the other B-Jacobian elements: the other subspaces of the SCD derivative (Gfrerer and Outrata, *Variational inequalities of the second kind*, 2022: for a Lipschitz map these subspaces are the graphs of the B-Jacobian matrices) and both branches of the limiting coderivative in nonsmooth adjoint inclusions (Suonperä and Valkonen, *Single-loop approaches to nonsmooth bilevel optimisation*, 2026, where the constraint set of the adjoint is a union of two convex sets). ## 1.x — candidates

Additions the frozen ABI allows, not yet scheduled.

- [x] Spectral prox maps on matrices: the nuclear norm, the spectral-norm ball, projection onto positive semidefinite matrices, and any elementwise operator applied to the singular values or eigenvalues (moved from version 2 on 2026-10-20). The caller supplies the decomposition (NumPy, PyTorch or JAX `svd`/`eigh`, on the CPU or the GPU), so the C core stays free of LAPACK and BLAS (spec Section 8). The core applies an existing scalar prox to the spectrum and gives the JVP and VJP of the spectral map by the Daleckii–Krein divided differences: repeated singular values and values at a kink, where autograd through an SVD returns infinite or wrong gradients, are handled exactly. The Python API gets `fp.Spectral(op)` and `fp.NuclearNorm(lam)`, and PyTorch and JAX wrappers as for image TV. — Design (2026-10-20): docs/spec.md, Section 5, "Spectral operators". The core returns only k × k results: from the spectrum s, its prox z and A = Uᵀ H V it gives B and the complement ratio r. The caller reassembles U B Vᵀ with its own BLAS and on its own device. The calls are `fp_op_spectral_check` and `fp_spectral_jvp`; the map is self-adjoint, so the same call gives the VJP. Ties and zeros take their limits from one JVP per tie group. The formula was checked numerically before review: central differences agree to 1e-9 on square, tall, tied and rank-deficient matrices, and adjointness holds to 1e-15. Autograd through `torch.linalg.svd` returns NaN at the tie. Progress (2026-10-20): the C core and the NumPy layer, `fp.Spectral` with `NuclearNorm`, `SpectralNormBall` and `PSD`, checked against finite differences at ties and zeros and against Candès et al.'s divergence. Then `fastprox.torch.prox` and `fastprox.jax.prox` on spectral operators, with the decomposition and products on the input's device and the k × k core on the CPU, checked by gradcheck and finite differences, at ties too. Later, if needed: a closed-form 2 × 2 and 3 × 2 SVD in the core for batches of tiny matrices (total nuclear variation), and a device path for B.
- [ ] Free-threaded CPython wheels (cp313t/cp314t): `FREE_THREADED` in `nanobind_add_module`, cibuildwheel for the free-threaded targets, a `PYTHON_GIL=0` test job (the suite, the context-lock test, an 8-thread scaling check), then the classifier. The C side already serves concurrent callers with the GIL released (audit P12, docs/audit-2026-10.md#p12)

**Analysis priors** (design 2026-10-23: docs/spec.md, Section 5, "Analysis priors: smoothed TV, second-order TV and learned potentials"), in stage order:

- [ ] Stage 1, Huber-smoothed TV: `fp_op_tv_iso_huber`, `fp_op_tv_aniso_huber`, `TVIsoHuber`, `TVAnisoHuber`. Sum of α_p h_δ(‖(W D z)_p‖), with h_δ the Huber function of `fp_op_huber`, 2D and 3D, with pixel or edge weights and θ ending in δ. The isotropic kernel's dual with a strongly convex conjugate, by V-FISTA (Beck, Section 10.7.7) at κ = 1 + γ α_max L / δ, so the rate is linear. The same gap certificate, ‖z − z*‖² ≤ 2·gap. Derivatives by conjugate gradients on (I + Gᵀ H G) with H bounded by γα/δ: no flat regions, no union-find, the H-apply kernel shared with the isotropic derivative's cap. δ can be learned. Device path in the PyTorch and JAX wrappers. Gate: the gap recomputed independently; ‖z_δ − z_TV‖² ≤ γδΣα_p against `TVIso` and `TVAniso`; iterations within a constant of √κ log(1/tol); finite differences in x, γ, the weights and δ; faster than `TVIso` to every tolerance at δ ≥ 1e-3 on the 0.2 benchmark images
- [ ] Stage 2, spline potentials: `fp_op_spline`, `fp_op_group_spline`, `Spline`, `GroupSpline`. φ′ continuous piecewise linear through given knots, plus an optional ℓ1 kink at 0, so Huber, the elastic net and learned ridge potentials are special cases. The prox is exact by a binary search over the pieces. Weakly convex potentials need γρ max w < 1 (FP_EPARAM otherwise, as MCP). Derivatives in x, γ, the weights, the knot values and the kink, with kink masks and branches. Gate: grid minimisation as for the nonconvex maps, Huber and the elastic net reproduced to rounding, an entry in `CASES`
- [ ] Stage 3, filter-bank analysis prior and second-order TV: `fp_op_analysis`, `fp_op_tv2`, `Analysis`, `TV2`. Groups of small filters (up to 9 taps per axis) with a potential per group (|t|, Huber, elastic net, convex spline) and pixel weights. The same dual method with L = Σ‖w_f‖₁² and the same gap. Derivatives in the filters, the pixel weights and the potentials' parameters. With flat entries, MINRES on the constrained system, measured against the capped system first. Second-order TV is the Frobenius Hessian norm by forward second differences (L = 64 in 2D, 144 in 3D). Gate: forward differences as filters reproduce `TVIso` and `TVAniso` within the gaps; second-order TV matches CVXPY, and affine images are fixed points; finite differences in the filters and coefficients
- [ ] Stage 4, weakly convex potentials in the analysis prior (WCRR): the primal accelerated gradient on the m-strongly convex prox objective, m = 1 − γρα_max L > 0, reporting gap = ‖∇P‖²/(2m) (so ‖z − z*‖² ≤ 2·gap/m). Gate: against a long reference solve; derivatives by conjugate gradients against finite differences

**Coderivatives for nonsmooth bilevel methods** (design 2026-10-23: docs/spec.md, Section 7, "Coderivatives for nonsmooth bilevel methods"), for single-loop methods and for adjoint methods that choose an element at kinks:

- [ ] Family 1, balls of a dual or a conjugate: `fp_workspace_multipliers` and `fp_project_coderiv` (`ctx.multipliers`, `op.project_coderivative`). After `fp_prox_conj` of group ℓ2 and soft thresholding, and after `fp_prox` of isotropic, anisotropic and analysis-prior TV. The multiplier ν (g = ν u at the solution; y = (1 + ν) u for a conjugate projection, Suonperä and Valkonen 2026, Remark 6.2), the codes inside, active and degenerate, and the projection onto the Fréchet (5.21) or limiting (5.22) set per ball. Before implementing: settle the difference between (5.18) and (5.22) on {λ = 0, ⟨w, u⟩ < 0} (spec Section 7). Gate: the sets against the case analysis of Lemma 5.5, the variational inequality of each projection, determinism
- [ ] Family 2, elementwise prox maps at kinks: soft thresholding, the elastic net, Huber, box and orthant, ℓ1 over a box, SCAD, MCP and splines, and their group forms. The regular coderivative is the segment between the two one-sided pieces' adjoints, jointly in x, γ and θ, and empty when (b − a) y < 0 (Rockafellar and Wets, Proposition 8.37). The limiting one adds the endpoints. The projection onto its graph per entry also returns the mixture weight s, and `fp_workspace_set_branch_weights` (`branch_weights=` in Python) makes `fp_jvp` and `fp_vjp` differentiate the mixture, parameter gradients included. ℓ0, ℓq and the log penalty are refused at ties (unbounded coderivative). Gate: the sets against 8(26) on one-sided differences; the mixture's VJP between the one-sided VJPs; the empty case
- [ ] Family 3, polyhedral rows with interacting kinks (simplex, ℓ1 ball, hyperplane with box, isotonic regression, 1D, anisotropic, graph and tree TV): a small active-set QP per row over its m kink entries. Design only, scheduled when a user needs more than isolated kinks; an isolated kink is already family 2's segment
