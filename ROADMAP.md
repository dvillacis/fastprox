# Roadmap

Planned work, release by release, following [docs/spec.md](docs/spec.md)
(Section 13). Each release ships only when its gate passes. Finished work
moves to [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md); decisions that change the spec are
logged at the end of this file.

## 0.2 — images

- [x] Anisotropic TV in 2D and 3D (2026-10-16): `fp_op_tv_aniso`, `TVAniso`, `tv_aniso`. Two active axes: Beck's accelerated dual block method (ADBPG, §12.5.4, Thm 12.19; Chambolle–Pock 2015) with exact 1D TV steps; three: Beck's fast dual proximal gradient for three blocks (Algorithm 9), which beat parallel proximal Dykstra at every accuracy. Certified gap stopping, warm starts through the workspace dual, polish to the exact solution on the identified regions, and O(n) derivatives from those regions. On Apple M5 fastest from 1e-3 relative error on in 2D against proxTV's DR2, Kolmogorov2, Yang2 and parallel Dykstra, 3.0–3.7× faster than the best proxTV solver at 1e-6 ([docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md))
- [x] Anisotropic TV in 3D at high accuracy (2026-10-17): gradient restarts (O'Donoghue and Candès) in both solvers, and the polish rebuilt to take its regions from the dual (edges whose 1D dual is strictly inside its bound are flat at the solution). It now runs at the gap checks and stops the solve once the polished point certifies, with a gap at most 1/100 of the iterate's. On 64³ the time to 1e-6 relative error fell 4.7–5.6×, and fastprox is now 1.8× (λ = 0.1) and 4.8× (λ = 0.5) faster than Yang's ADMM, fastest at every accuracy. 2D to 1e-6 is 2.3–2.8× faster than before. The nested split (exact 1D on one axis, warm-started 2D ADBPG on the other two, as in Barbero–Sra's proximity stacking) was tried first and lost to the restarted three-block method at every accuracy ([docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md))
- [x] Feasible warm starts for anisotropic TV (2026-10-17): a starting dual is checked fiber by fiber and rebuilt from its clipped running sums where it is infeasible; before, a seed such as q₀ = x − mean(x) certified the constant image at 0 iterations
- [ ] Anisotropic TV to 1e-2 in 2D at small λ: proxTV's DR2 is still 17–20% quicker at λ = 0.1. Some medium accuracies (1e-3 at λ = 0.5, 1e-4) are also 7–19% slower than the old end-of-solve polish made them
- [x] Parallel polish for anisotropic TV (2026-10-17): union-find in fixed slabs along axis 0 (at most 64, of at least 4 planes or rows), each joined by its own thread, then the crossing edges serially; labels and region sums per slab, the crossing components serially. The output is bit-identical to the serial polish. The edge pass had not vectorised (its pointers lost `restrict` through a lambda) and is 5.6× faster. One polish on 64³: 7.1 → 6.5 ms on one thread, 3.9 → 1.8 ms on eight; whole solves to 1e-12 gain 1–6% on one thread and 3–15% on eight ([docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md))
- [x] Isotropic TV in 2D and 3D (2026-10-16): `fp_op_tv_iso`, `TVIso`, `tv_iso`. Fast gradient projection on the dual (Beck–Teboulle 2009; Beck 2017, §12.4.4) with step 1/L, L = 4 Σ wₐ² (8 in 2D, 12 in 3D), primal from the last dual iterate (12.19), gap stopping certifying ‖z − z*‖² ≤ 2·gap (Lemma 12.7), dual returned and accepted for warm starts. Per-axis weights inside the norm. Derivatives by conjugate gradients on the flat regions, with the residual reported. Against scikit-image's Chambolle projection on Apple M5: 2.6–11× faster from 1e-3 relative error on, and the only one of the two to reach 1e-6; scikit-image is 25–45% faster to 1e-2 at small γ ([docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md))
- [x] Isotropic TV at high accuracy (2026-10-17): gradient restarts (O'Donoghue and Candès, Found. Comput. Math. 15, 2015), tested every 4th iteration with a non-shrinking interval, take up to 15× fewer iterations to 1e-8 and below; the derivative CG is Jacobi-preconditioned (1.4–3× fewer iterations where it is expensive). The rate does not become linear, because the dual is degenerate. A Newton polish on the flat regions was tried and was worse than z(u) ([docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md))
- [ ] Isotropic TV at γ ≥ 1 to 1e-10: most test images still need over 20 000 restarted FISTA iterations. Candidates: a semismooth Newton method on the dual (Hintermüller and Stadler) or a primal-dual method with an adaptive step
- [x] 1D TV with ℓ2 of differences (2026-10-17): `fp_op_tv1d_l2`, `TV1DL2`, `tv1d_l2`. Newton on the secular equation of the dual trust-region problem (Moré–Sorensen; Nocedal–Wright Alg. 4.4) instead of projected Newton, exact to rounding in 2–8 tridiagonal steps, with closed-form derivatives; 1.0–1.3× faster than proxTV's `more_TV2`, which hits its iteration cap on many large rows ([docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md))
- [x] Nonconvex pointwise maps (2026-10-16): ℓ0, ℓq (closed forms for q = 1/2 and 2/3, Newton from |x| otherwise, monotone on the nonzero branch), SCAD and MCP for any step (a > 1 + γ, b > γ, else `FP_EPARAM`), log penalty. Verified against minimisation on a refined grid in `tests/python/test_nonconvex.py`
- [x] Tie rule (`fp_options.tie`, nonzero branch by default) and tie counts in `fp_info.ties` (2026-10-16): exact at the double-precision threshold, the same for any thread count
- [x] Derivatives for the nonconvex maps (diagonal, support-restricted, from the scalar equation; the selected branch at ties) (2026-10-16)
- [x] Cheap exact operators found in the literature review of 2026-10-15, each reusing an existing kernel or closed form with a simple Jacobian (pages are the book's printed pages):
  - [x] prox of max(x) as x − λ P_Δ(x/λ) (Beck Ex. 6.49, p. 162; Parikh and Boyd, *Proximal Algorithms*, §6.4.1, whose bisection interval is right only for λ = 1), and of the sum of the k largest entries or absolute values through the capped simplex (Beck Ex. 6.50–6.51) (2026-10-16: `Max`, `SumLargest`, on the set scaled by γλ)
  - [x] projection onto a hyperplane intersected with a box, P_Box(x − μa) with μ a root of a monotone piecewise-linear function (Beck Thm 6.27, pp. 147–148), which contains the simplex and the capped simplex; Jacobian I_F − a_F a_Fᵀ/‖a_F‖² on the free set (2026-10-16: `Hyperplane`, `HalfSpace`; Newton steps in a bracket with a median fallback, 3–9 ns per entry)
  - [x] projection onto a level set {f ≤ α}, e.g. a 1D TV ball, as prox_{γf} with γ the root of f(prox_{γf}(x)) = α (Beck Thm 6.30), using ∂z/∂γ for Newton steps and the implicit derivative (2026-10-16: `TV1DBall`, 4–15 1D TV solves per row; other level sets when a need appears)
  - [x] group Huber, (1 − λ / max(‖x‖, μ + λ)) x (Beck Ex. 6.66), and radial lifting of any scalar prox to a group one (Beck Thm 6.18), e.g. group SCAD and MCP (2026-10-16: `GroupHuber`, `GroupSCAD`, `GroupMCP`, one kernel around the scalar operator)
  - [x] half-space, hyperplane, second-order cone (Beck Ex. 6.37), −λ Σ log xᵢ as (x + √(x² + 4λ))/2 (Beck Lemma 6.5), ℓ1 over a box (Beck Ex. 6.23) (half-space and hyperplane done 2026-10-16, with an optional box; `SOC`, `LogBarrier`, `L1Box` 2026-10-16)
  - [x] sparse group lasso and tree-structured group norms by composing soft thresholding and group proxes in tree order (Bach, Jenatton, Mairal and Obozinski, *Optimization with Sparsity-Inducing Penalties*, (3.8)) (2026-10-16: `TreeL2` and `TreeL2.sparse_group`, groups in any order)
- [x] Python: `fp.lq`, `fp.tv_iso` returning `(z, info)` with `info.gap` and `info.dual`, and the other new operators (2026-10-16; `fp.tv_aniso`, `TVAniso`, `TVIso`, `L0`, `Lq`, `SCAD`, `MCP`, `LogPenalty` and their one-shot functions)
- [x] Check the citations marked "to check" in the spec (2026-10-16): Beck–Teboulle against Beck (2017), Appendix D; Xu et al., Cao–Sun–Xu, Fan–Li and Zhang against the bibliographies of Wen et al. (IEEE Access 2018) and a second citing paper, now listed as checked in docs/spec.md with volume and pages. The originals were not opened, so the kernel derives every closed form (SCAD and MCP also for γ ≠ 1) and the tests verify it against grid minimisation
- [x] Per-entry weights for SCAD and MCP (2026-10-17): g(z) = Σ wᵢ φ(zᵢ) with `fp_op_scad(λ, a, w)`, `fp_op_mcp(λ, b, w)`, `SCAD(lam, a, weights)`, `MCP(lam, b, weights)`. Entry i is the unweighted map at the step γwᵢ (SCAD is not a reparametrised SCAD, but its prox depends on the step only through γwᵢ; for MCP this is w·MCP_{λ,b} = MCP_{wλ,b/w}), with the checks b > γ max w and a > 1 + γ max w. θ = (λ, a or b, w…) with weights and (λ, a or b) without, so unweighted callers and the group penalties keep their layout; the VJP in w is the hypergradient for edge weights. Unweighted maps are bit-identical and as fast as before; weighted ones cost 1.0–1.3 ns per entry. `ElemDeriv` now carries three parameter derivatives per entry
- [x] Kink mask for the elementwise operators (2026-10-17), the first part of the 0.3 kink and tie mask item, brought forward for the SCD paper: soft thresholding, ℓ1 over a box, SCAD and MCP. `fp_workspace_kinks(ws, tol, mask, &count)` gives an int8 code per entry (new dtype `FP_I8`): which breakpoint lies within `tol`, absolute in the input's units. `fp_workspace_set_branch(ws, side, tol)` makes `fp_jvp`/`fp_vjp` take the piece on the x − ε or x + ε side of it, with the piece's formula at x. In Python: `ctx.kinks(tol)`, and `branch=`/`kink_tol=` on `jvp`/`vjp`. Gate met: the mask is the same for any thread count, and each side's JVP matches the one-sided finite difference from its side, in x and along the piece in γ and θ. Each map gained explicit pieces; default derivatives are bit-identical and as fast as before
- [ ] Jacobian diagonal of the elementwise operators in Python, `ctx.jacobian_diag()` (planned 2026-10-17): the same as `jvp(ctx, dx=ones)` but named, and honouring the branch override. A semismooth* Newton step reads the SCD subspace of each entry from it (B = 0: entry fixed at zero; B = 1: no curvature; otherwise the slope of the concave branch)
- [x] Example `examples/weighted_glasso.py` (2026-10-17): weighted graphical lasso by ADMM, with the −log det step as `eigh` plus `LogBarrier` on the eigenvalues and the penalty step as weighted `Soft` or weighted `MCP` on the packed upper triangle (each pair counted twice in the penalty and in the Frobenius term, so the per-entry prox at step 1/r solves the usual Σ_{i≠j} form). The graphical lasso, adaptive weights from a first estimate and weighted MCP compared on edge recovery and error, with the KKT residual of the convex solves; run by the Python tests

**Gate:** iterative operators meet `gap ≤ tol · ½‖x‖²` with the gap recomputed independently in tests; nonconvex maps verified against a dense grid with refinement, tie flags exact at thresholds.

## 0.3 — graph TV

- [ ] Max-flow engine (Boykov–Kolmogorov style, tree reuse between levels), written from the paper (clean room)
- [ ] Graph TV by parametric max-flow, exact to a user-set precision (Chambolle–Darbon, Hochbaum)
- [ ] TV on trees and chains (Kolmogorov, Pock and Rolínek)
- [ ] VJPs for the iterative operators by conjugate gradients on the dual active set, returning the residual. Choose the active set from the strict support of the computed primal and handle ties within the tolerance explicitly: picking an index set by rule can give a matrix outside the generalised Jacobian (Outrata, Kočvara and Zowe, *Nonsmooth Approach to Optimization Problems with Equilibrium Constraints*, Ex. 7.3)
- [ ] Kink and tie mask (the elementwise part with kinks is done, see 0.2; ties, the other operators and the image operators remain): report which entries sat at a kink of the prox or on a tie, not only the count in `fp_info.ties`. The elementwise operators come first, in 0.2 (see there). Kinks are the biactive entries of each operator: |xᵢ| = γλ for soft thresholding, the breakpoints of SCAD and MCP, an entry exactly on a bound of the box, an entry at the simplex threshold, a 1D TV edge whose dual sits exactly at ±γwₑ, a group whose norm equals its threshold; for the image operators, the pixels where the dual is on the boundary of its ball with a zero jump. An optional tolerance flags near-kinks as well, since exact equality is rare in floating point; the iterative VJPs above need the same rule for ties within the tolerance. It crosses the C API through the workspace like `fp_workspace_deriv_info`, written only with `keep_structure` (`fp_prox` keeps its signature), and in Python as `ctx.kinks`. `jvp` and `vjp` take an optional branch override for the flagged entries, so a caller can build the other B-Jacobian elements: the other subspaces of the SCD derivative (Gfrerer and Outrata, *Variational inequalities of the second kind*, 2022: for a Lipschitz map these subspaces are the graphs of the B-Jacobian matrices) and both branches of the limiting coderivative in nonsmooth adjoint inclusions (Suonperä and Valkonen, *Single-loop approaches to nonsmooth bilevel optimisation*, 2026, where the constraint set of the adjoint is a union of two convex sets). Gate: the mask is the same for any thread count, and for every flagged entry each branch's JVP matches the one-sided finite difference from its side
- [ ] Spatially varying weights for the image operators: one weight per pixel for isotropic TV, Σ_p α_p ‖(wₐ Dₐ z)_p‖₂, and one per pixel and axis for anisotropic TV (per edge, as 1D TV already has), on top of the per-axis weights. The operator is built for one image shape, so θ has a fixed length as for weighted 1D TV, and the VJP returns a gradient per pixel or edge. Needed for learning spatially dependent regularization parameters in bilevel problems, and for iteratively reweighted ℓ1 (Ochs, Dosovitskiy, Brox and Pock, SIAM J. Imaging Sci. 8, 2015, 331–372), whose convex majorizers of TV^q are weighted TV problems. Isotropic: the weights enter only the dual constraint ‖u_p‖₂ ≤ γα_p, so the step and the warm-start projection carry over; zero weights decouple pixels. Anisotropic: the 1D steps use the weighted Condat kernel, and the region derivatives take the weight of each boundary edge. New constructors, so the existing ones keep their meaning. Gate: CVXPY fixtures with random weights, finite differences on the weight gradients, and an example that runs reweighted ℓ1 for TV^q to a stationary point
- [ ] PyTorch (`torch.autograd.Function`) and JAX (`custom_vjp` + `pure_callback`) wrappers, checked with `gradcheck`
- [ ] Device-native image TV in those wrappers: isotropic and anisotropic TV in 2D and 3D written in framework tensor operations, so it runs on the input's device, GPU included, with no change to the C API. Dual fast gradient projection with the C kernels' gap stopping and warm starts (ℓ2-ball projection of the dual for isotropic TV, clamping for anisotropic), and a backward by conjugate gradients on the flat regions, so memory does not grow with the iteration count. The exact 1D TV steps and region averaging of the anisotropic C kernel stay on the CPU. Reproducible run to run on one device and build, not bit-identical to the CPU (see the decisions table). Gate: `gradcheck`, the gap recomputed in tests, agreement with the C kernels within the gap tolerance
- [ ] Benchmark the device-native path against DeepInverse's TV denoiser (Chambolle–Pock, autograd through the iterations) on CPU and GPU: time to a given accuracy, the cost of host transfers for the C path, and memory of a backward pass through an unrolled network. With the numbers, open an issue upstream proposing an optional backend; if transfers dominate at typical image sizes, move the 1.x CUDA kernels for image TV forward

- [ ] First operators, chosen from what the 0.3 device path shows people running on GPUs: iterative image TV (anisotropic with region labelling for its derivatives), elementwise maps, batched simplex and ℓ1-ball projections, and exact 1D TV with one thread per row for large batches. - [ ] Packaging: CUDA builds as a separate package rather than in the main wheel, and through conda-forge
